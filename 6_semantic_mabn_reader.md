1. The Schrödinger-Everett Foundation: Continuous Possibility

In 1926, Erwin Schrödinger gave physics its most critical quantum tool: the Schrödinger Equation. This formula calculates the wave function ($\Psi$), a mathematical description of a physical system that does not map hard, distinct, localized objects, but rather a fluid spectrum of overlapping potentials and probabilities. According to the foundational math, a subatomic particle does not exist in one definitive place; it exists as a coherent cloud of possibilities.
The institutional interpretation of this math—the Copenhagen Interpretation—introduced a clumsy, dualistic patch: the assertion that when a conscious human observer looks at the particle, the wave function mysteriously and instantly “collapses” into a single, classical reality.
In 1957, Hugh Everett III challenged this institutional consensus by asking a parsimonious question: What if the wave function never collapses? Everett demonstrated that if you apply Schrödinger’s equation continuously to the entire cosmos, you do not need a magical, external collapse mechanism. Instead, whenever a quantum event encounters multiple potential outcomes, the universal wave function splits.
The Legacy Bottleneck: The Solipsism Paradox

Everett’s pure mathematical proof that the universal wave function is continuous and non-collapsing remains bulletproof. However, his Many-Worlds Interpretation—the physical mechanics he proposed to explain it—introduces a severe structural vulnerability. Everett assumed that the universe physically splits into an infinite, branching multiverse of independent parallel planets.
When we evaluate this from an informational perspective, we encounter the Solipsism Paradox (The Coordination Problem). If your localized consciousness branches down Timeline A, and my localized consciousness branches down Timeline B, why do we continue to interact within a highly stable, shared, and identical macro-reality?
For billions of independent biological observers to render the exact same objective physical world without drifting into isolated, private universes, an infinite multiverse would require an unfathomably complex, unproven master-synchronization protocol. Relying on an infinite duplication of physical matter violates the law of parsimony. Therefore, while we preserve Everett’s math of a non-collapsing, continuous universal wave function, we completely evict his mechanism of splitting physical worlds.
