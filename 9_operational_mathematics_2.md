Part 2: Running the Math on the Core Testable Problem

To test Boyer’s November Operational Mathematics equation in a real lab, we have to plug in actual physical constants for a single living cell (such as an E. coli bacterium or a single mammalian neuron) and see what the numbers demand.

The Governing Equation (from Working Paper v7.0)

F_crit = 1 − Ṡ_max / (k_B · J(t) · ln 2)

Or, written in terms of Heat Power (Watts = Joules/second) by multiplying top and bottom by body temperature T:

F_crit = 1 − Q̇_max / (k_B · T · J(t) · ln 2)

Step 1: Plug in Known Physical Constants

Boltzmann Constant (k_B): 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K

Temperature (T): 310 K (human body temp, 37°C)

Natural Log of 2 (ln 2): ≈ 0.693

Landauer Minimum Heat per Bit (k_B · T · ln 2):
(1.38 × 10⁻²³) × 310 × 0.693 ≈ 2.97 × 10⁻²¹ Joules per bit

Step 2: Plug in Real Biological Numbers (Single Mammalian Cell)

Maximum Metabolic Heat Clearance (Q̇_max): A typical active mammalian cell (like a neuron or liver cell) generates and clears roughly 10⁻¹¹ Watts (10 picowatts, or 10⁻¹¹ Joules/second) of heat.

What Information Flux Rate (J(t)) is required to match that heat?
Let’s solve for the bit rate J(t) needed for a complete loss of fidelity (F = 0) to equal the cell’s 10 picowatt thermal budget:

J(t) = Q̇_max / (k_B · T · ln 2) = (10⁻¹¹ J/s) / (2.97 × 10⁻²¹ J/bit) ≈ 3.37 × 10⁹ bits/second (3.37 Gigabits/sec)

Step 3: Compare That Number to Reality

Here is what that number tells us—and why it is so fascinating:

At the Classic Macro-Receptor Level (Too Small): Standard biochemical signaling (like an E. coli sensing sugar gradients via surface receptors) only processes about 10 to 100 bits/second. At 100 bits/sec, the Landauer heat tax is only 3 × 10⁻¹⁹ Watts—roughly 30 million times smaller than the cell’s normal metabolic heat, making it completely invisible to any calorimeter.

At the Microtubule / Molecular Level (Right on Target!): A single mammalian neuron contains roughly 10⁷ to 10⁸ tubulin dimers inside its microtubule network. If those tubulin states oscillate or update at even a modest biological frequency (say, 100 Hz to 1 kHz), the internal structural information flux J(t) is:

10⁷ dimers × 100 Hz = 10⁹ bits/second (1 Gigabit/sec)

The Bottom-Line Takeaway from the Math

If J(t) is only cell-surface chemistry (hundreds of bits/sec), the Landauer tax is negligible—standard biochemistry wins, and the thermal flash is undetectable.

If J(t) includes sub-cellular cytoskeletal/microtubule states (~1 to 10 Gigabits/sec), the Landauer heat tax lands within a single order of magnitude of the cell’s actual metabolic power budget (10⁻¹¹ W)—meaning a sudden drop in fidelity F(t) would produce a measurable picowatt-scale thermal spike right at F_crit!

2. Checking Real-World Instrumentation Limits

Can current lab equipment actually see that 10 picowatt threshold inside a single living cell? Comparing Boyer’s proposed sensors against published experimental benchmarks gives a clear verdict:

Microfluidic Chip Calorimeters

Published Sensitivity: 4 pW to 200 pW (0.2 nW in suspended microfluidic chips; ~4–5 pW in vacuum-isolated resistive/cantilever chips)

Target Signal Needed: ~10 pW (mammalian cell budget)

Lab Verdict: Borderline / Feasible. A 10 pW spike sits right above the ~4–5 pW noise floor of top research-grade picocalorimeters, though fluid movement and baseline metabolic drift make isolating a sub-second flash extremely tricky.

Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) Nanodiamond Sensors

Published Sensitivity: ~50 mK to 100 mK inside living cells (down to ~1.6 mK/√Hz in hybrid ensembles)

Target Signal Needed: < 0.001 mK (microkelvin scale for a 10 pW cellular pulse)

Lab Verdict: Currently Too Insensitive for Heat Alone. Because water conducts heat rapidly at the nanoscale, a 10 pW pulse inside a single cell only raises local temperature by microkelvins—well below the ~50–100 mK intracellular thermal noise floor of nanodiamonds.

NV Diamond Spin / Magnetic Sensing

Published Sensitivity: ~63 nT/√Hz (single-spin magnetometry capable of detecting individual electron/radical spin flips)

Target Signal Needed: Direct quantum coherence / spin relaxation time (T₂)

Lab Verdict: Best Experimental Route. Rather than measuring heat with nanodiamonds, researchers can use NV centers to directly measure spin decoherence inside microtubules—testing whether F(t) drops sharply right before death.

The Bottom Line on Lab Feasibility

The Calorimetry Test is Possible, With Caveats: State-of-the-art vacuum-isolated chip calorimeters (~4–5 pW resolution) can resolve a 10 pW shift, provided the cell’s baseline ATP metabolism is chemically clamped so ordinary chemical heat doesn’t mask the informational spike.

The Nanodiamond Role Needs Tweaking: Intracellular nanodiamond thermometry (~50–100 mK precision) isn’t sensitive enough to catch the tiny temperature bump of a Landauer flash in water, but NV-diamond spin magnetometry is ideal for tracking the coherence drop F(t) itself.
